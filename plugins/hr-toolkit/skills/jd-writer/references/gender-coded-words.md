# Gender-coded words in job ads

Source basis: Gaucher, Friesen & Kay (2011), "Evidence that gendered wording in job advertisements exists and sustains gender inequality", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. The study found masculine-coded wording made jobs less appealing to women and reduced their sense of belonging; it did not find feminine-coded wording put men off to the same degree. Later studies show mixed effect sizes, so treat this list as a prompt for balance, not a rulebook.

How to use it:
- Match word stems (`compet*` catches competitive, competition).
- Report what you found and the overall skew. Replace masculine-coded words that aren't essential; don't replace every word, and don't swing to a heavy feminine skew.
- Context matters: "analytical" in a data-analyst JD is a real requirement. Keep it and balance elsewhere.
- The list is for English. For other languages, also check grammatical gender: use both forms or a neutral form in job titles (e.g. German "(m/w/d)" convention), as local norms or law may expect.

## Masculine-coded (stems)

active, adventur*, aggress*, ambitio*, analy*, assert*, athlet*, autonom*, boast*, challeng*, compet*, confident, courag*, decisi*, determin*, dominan*, force*, greedy, headstrong, hierarch*, hostil*, impulsive, independen*, individual*, intellect*, lead*, logic, masculine, objective, opinion, outspoken, persist, principle*, reckless, self-confiden*, self-relian*, self-sufficien*, stubborn, superior, unreasonab*

## Feminine-coded (stems)

affectionate, cheer*, commit*, communal, compassion*, connect*, considerate, cooperat*, depend*, emotiona*, empath*, feminine, flatterable, gentle, honest, interdependen*, interpersona*, kind, kinship, loyal*, modesty, nag, nurtur*, pleasant*, polite, quiet*, respon*, sensitiv*, submissive, support*, sympath*, tender*, together*, trust*, understand*, warm*, whin*, yield*

## Hype and exclusionary terms (remove regardless of gender coding)

| Term | Problem | Try instead |
|---|---|---|
| rockstar, ninja, guru, wizard, superhero, hacker (as praise) | Vague; skews young and male in perception | the real title: "senior engineer" |
| dominate, crush, kill it | Aggressive tone | "deliver", "grow", "build" |
| work hard, play hard / beer Fridays | Signals a narrow social culture | describe real benefits and flexibility |
| digital native, recent graduate, young, energetic team | Age proxy (graduate schemes may be lawful in some places: check locally) | "comfortable with [specific tools]" |
| native speaker | National-origin proxy | "fluent / professional proficiency in X" |
| culture fit | Undefined; invites bias | name the values or behaviours you need |
| he / his / guys | Excludes | you / they / team |
| manpower, chairman, salesman | Gendered | staffing, chair, salesperson |
| able-bodied, must be able to stand/lift (when not essential) | Disability proxy | state essential physical demands only, with "adjustments available" |
| crazy, insane (pace) | Ableist and vague | describe the actual pace or workload |

## Neutral swaps for common masculine-coded words

| Instead of | Consider |
|---|---|
| aggressive targets | stretching targets, or state the number |
| dominant market position | largest / well-established market position |
| competitive environment | high-performing team; or remove |
| decisive | makes clear, timely decisions |
| independent / self-reliant | can manage own priorities |
| fearless | willing to try new approaches |
| strong (communicator) | clear (communicator) |
