# Policy checklists: what to cover and what to verify

Each policy type lists (a) content the policy normally needs and (b) "verify with legal" prompts. The prompts are questions, not statements of law. Laws named here are examples of where rules commonly come from, accurate as general orientation only; their scope, thresholds and dates change and must be checked for each jurisdiction.

Universal verify items for every policy:
- Does a collective agreement, works council or employee-representative body need to be consulted or agree before this policy changes (common in e.g. Germany, France, the Netherlands)?
- Does local law require the policy, or parts of it, in a particular language?
- Is the policy contractual or non-contractual, and what does the employment contract say?
- Data protection: does the procedure process personal or sensitive data (health, family, biometrics)? Is a privacy notice or impact assessment needed?

## Leave (annual, sick, parental, bereavement, carer's, etc.)
Cover: types of leave, entitlement and accrual, how to request, notice, pay during leave, carry-over, interaction with public holidays, return-to-work, records.
Verify:
- Statutory minimum for each leave type, and whether it's paid, in each location.
- Eligibility thresholds (length of service, hours, headcount, e.g. US FMLA applies only to employers above a size threshold).
- Protection from dismissal or detriment during and after leave.
- Pay-out of unused leave at termination; carry-over limits.
- Rules on medical evidence for sick leave (when it can be required, who can see it).
- Inclusive parental leave definitions (adoption, surrogacy, all parents) and any statutory differences between them.

## Remote / hybrid work
Cover: eligibility, required office presence, how to request patterns, equipment, expenses, working hours and availability, security, working from another country, health and safety, review of arrangements.
Verify:
- Statutory rights to request flexible working and required response process (e.g. UK).
- Health-and-safety duties for home workers.
- Working-time, right-to-disconnect and recording rules (some countries have these).
- Mandatory reimbursement of home-working costs (some US states, e.g. California, require reimbursing necessary business expenses).
- Cross-border remote work: tax, social security, permanent-establishment and immigration consequences.
- Disability and caring-responsibility adjustments to office-presence rules.

## Expenses and travel
Cover: what is reimbursable, approval limits, booking rules, per diems, receipts, claim deadlines, corporate cards, misuse.
Verify:
- Tax treatment of allowances and per diems in each country.
- Legal duty to reimburse certain costs (varies by place).
- Record-keeping periods for receipts.
- Duty-of-care obligations for travelling employees.

## Code of conduct
Cover: values as behaviours, conflicts of interest, gifts and hospitality, confidentiality, use of company assets, social media, outside work, speaking up, consequences.
Verify:
- Whistleblowing/speak-up protections and required reporting channels (e.g. EU Whistleblower Directive as transposed nationally; local laws elsewhere).
- Anti-bribery rules for gifts (e.g. UK Bribery Act, US FCPA where relevant).
- Limits on regulating off-duty conduct and social-media use, and protection of lawful collective/concerted activity (e.g. US NLRA rules on employee communications).

## Anti-harassment and bullying
Cover: definitions with examples (including sexual harassment and harassment related to any protected characteristic), scope (incl. third parties, remote and social events), reporting routes (more than one, including one outside the line manager), investigation steps, confidentiality, interim measures, no retaliation, support, training, outcomes.
Verify:
- Mandatory structures or bodies, e.g. India's POSH Act requires an Internal Committee in workplaces above a headcount threshold, and mandates specific procedures and timelines.
- Preventive duties, e.g. the UK's duty to prevent sexual harassment ("reasonable steps" since October 2024; the Employment Rights Act 2025 strengthens it to "all reasonable steps" and adds a third-party harassment duty, scheduled from late October 2026; confirm what is in force).
- Mandatory training requirements (some US states such as California and New York require periodic training).
- Required notices, posters or timelines for investigation.
- Protected characteristics list in each jurisdiction (it differs).

## Grievance and disciplinary
Cover: informal stage, formal stages, investigation, hearings, right to be accompanied, outcomes, appeals, timelines, records.
Verify:
- Statutory codes of practice that tribunals take into account (e.g. UK Acas Code).
- Right to be accompanied and by whom.
- Notice, warning and dismissal procedure requirements (strict in many countries, e.g. much of the EU and India's standing orders where applicable).

## Data, devices and monitoring
Cover: acceptable use, personal use, security, BYOD, what is monitored and why, retention, access to data, consequences.
Verify:
- Lawful basis and transparency for monitoring; impact assessment (e.g. GDPR DPIA).
- Consultation or consent requirements for monitoring (works councils; some US states require notice of electronic monitoring).
- Cross-border data transfer rules.

## AI use at work
Cover: approved and prohibited tools, what data may never be entered (personal data, confidential and client information, credentials), human review of outputs, disclosure when AI produced work, intellectual property, use in people decisions (hiring, performance) requiring extra controls, training, how to request a new tool.
Verify:
- Data-protection rules on entering personal data into third-party tools.
- AI-specific laws where AI is used for employment decisions: e.g. the EU AI Act lists recruitment, promotion, termination, task allocation and performance monitoring as high-risk (those obligations were postponed in 2026 to December 2027 at the earliest; check the current date); New York City Local Law 144 (bias audits and notices for automated employment decision tools); state laws such as Illinois's rules on AI in employment decisions and video interviews, and Colorado's AI law (the original 2024 Act was replaced in 2026 by a narrower automated-decision law due to start in 2027; check current status).
- Contractual confidentiality obligations to clients.
- Intellectual-property ownership of AI-assisted work.
